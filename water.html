Water

Temper hot water with cold to a set temperature, and mix brewing water from two concentrates.

Hot and cold mix

Mixing is worked out by heat balance on weight. Hot water is about 4% less dense than cold, so by volume the jug figures are adjusted for that. Vessel loss: how much the mix drops when poured into your jug or kettle. Take it from one trial: mix, stir, read, and enter the shortfall.

Mineral recipe

All values are ppm as CaCO₃.

Concentrates (make once, 1 litre each)

Buffer: 1.68 g baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) in 1 L distilled or RO water = 1000 ppm alkalinity.
Hardness: 2.45 g Epsom salt (magnesium sulphate) in 1 L distilled or RO water = 1000 ppm hardness.
Use food grade salts. Keep the bottles closed and in the fridge.

Starting water

Test strips or a drop kit give GH and KH. If a kit reads in dH, multiply by 17.8.

Mix

Check against SCA water ranges

Ranges from the SCA water quality handbook: hardness 17 to 85 (ideal about 68), alkalinity 10 to 100 (ideal about 40). Magnesium tends to extract more flavour than calcium; buffer softens acidity.
