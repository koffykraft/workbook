Learn coffee

From the plant to the cup, in order: short notes here, the app page that helps with each step, and the full KoffyKraft courses for going deeper. Figures are common ranges; your beans, roaster and water come first.
How to use the app
1 Grow
  • Arabica and Robusta want different heights, shade and care; the variety you plant sets much of the cup.
  • Living soil, shade trees and timely rain (blossom and backing showers) decide the crop more than any single input.
In the app: Crop care · Origin › varieties and words · Field log · Weather. Course: Living Root Network natural farming handbook.
2 Pick and process
  • Pick ripe cherry only; under-ripe and over-ripe fruit show up in the cup as harsh or fermented notes.
  • Washed, natural and honey are three ways of removing the fruit; each changes sweetness, body and acidity.
  • Dry slowly and evenly to a stable moisture, then rest the parchment or cherry before hulling.
In the app: Journey of the bean · Processing runs · Harvests and green lots.
3 Roast
  • Drying: the green bean loses water and turns from green to yellow. Even heat here sets up an even roast.
  • Browning: sugars and amino acids react (the Maillard reactions) and the bean turns tan to brown; most aroma starts here.
  • First crack: steam and gas split the bean with an audible crack. Ground colour at first crack sits around L* 30 on a colour meter, much the same for every arabica studied.
  • Development: the time from first crack to the end of the roast. Longer brings more sweetness and body and less acidity; too long and the coffee turns flat or bitter.
  • Second crack (around L* 20) marks dark roasts: oil comes to the surface and roast flavour takes over from origin flavour.
  • Cool fast to stop the roast, then judge the colour on ground coffee.
In the app: Timer · Graph · Roastbook · Colour check.
Course: What changes during roasting · First crack and development · Roast profiles · Roast defects · Light, medium and dark · all 25 lessons.
Colour points: UC Davis, A universal color curve for roasted arabica coffee (Scientific Reports, 2025).
4 Rest and store
  • Fresh roast gives off gas for days; most filter coffee tastes best after a few days of rest, espresso often a little longer.
  • Keep beans away from air, light, heat and damp: a valve bag or airtight jar, whole bean, ground just before brewing.
Course: Post-roast rest · Storage and packaging.
5 Cup and taste
  • Cupping is the same recipe for every coffee, so differences come from the coffee, not the brew.
  • Smell the dry grounds, break the crust, then taste as it cools: sweetness, acidity, body, aftertaste and any defect.
  • Cup each roast of the same lot side by side to see what a change in the roast did.
In the app: Cup. Course: Cupping and sensory foundations.
6 Brew
  • Ratio, grind and water temperature are the three dials; change one at a time.
  • Water is most of the cup: clean, not too hard and not too soft.
In the app: Brew guide · Brew log · Water. Course: Coffee brewing foundations.
